# Decision page: CBAM Exposure Case

**Question.** A 600 t/yr Indian fastener maker asks what to do about CBAM before 2034.

**Answer.** Buy verified rod from your current mill and verify your own plant. Start now, keep low-carbon rod as the next step, and stay in the EU.

| Position | Present-value hit, 2027-34 |
|---|---|
| Verified rod, current mill | EUR 160k |
| Low-carbon rod | EUR 417k |
| Verify own plant only | EUR 879k |
| Stay on defaults | EUR 1.06M |
| Sell outside the EU | EUR 1.31M |

Hit is what the exporter absorbs after a Taiwanese rival's own default-route bill, plus fixed costs, at a flat EUR 82.32/t and 12%. Verified rod is set at 3.06 tCO2e/t: the median of ten Indian plants in a draft government schedule, plus 0.1 for rolling.

## Why

1. **The bill is large now.** The "2.5% in 2026" shorthand holds only at the benchmark. Certificates are `embedded emissions - free allocation`, and free allocation is a benchmark amount. India's marked-up default for bolts is 4.6x that benchmark, so 79% of embedded emissions is charged in 2026. That is 4.96 certificates a tonne against 0.16 on the shorthand.
2. **The cost is heavy against the price.** On defaults, a tonne of bolts carries EUR 458 in 2027 (17% of the 2025 EU import price), rising to EUR 612 by 2034 (23%).
3. **Most of the saving sits in the rod.** Verifying the plant alone saves EUR 183k. Adding verified rod saves EUR 902k. 69% of the prize comes from the mill's report.
4. **Low-carbon rod comes second.** Free allocation falls with the cleaner route, which takes back 60% of the gain in 2027.

## Triggers

- Verified rod beats defaults above **62 t/yr** of EU sales. Verifying the plant alone needs 217 t/yr.
- Low-carbon rod beats verified rod if the mill is above **3.9 tCO2e/t**. All ten plants in the draft schedule sit below that line.
- It also wins in 2034 if carbon passes **EUR 87/t** at a EUR 179/t premium, close to today's price, so this needs another look around 2030.

## Outside checks

The European Fastener Distributor Association publishes the same formula and eight worked examples. Seven match this model to the cent. The eighth, an Indian line, matches on EFDA's benchmark of 1.364; the Commission's file marks that line stainless (1.154), which would give EUR 413.35 and which is left open here. Crowe UK's rough figures for Taiwan, Turkiye and China are within 10%.

## The EU buyer

On bolts CBAM cuts India's price gap to Taiwan from EUR 540 to EUR 267 a tonne and the order of suppliers stays the same. On hot-rolled plate India moves from number 3 to number 8 of 9 origins, cheapest first. Unit values mix grades, so these are guides, not quotes.

## Sector

India's in-scope exports to the EU were EUR 5.29bn in 2025 (4.32 Mt). On defaults that is EUR 1.16bn in 2026, 22% of value, against EUR 41M on the shorthand. FEPS-NIPFP estimated 16% of 2022 unit value and Sentra.world 10-20% of product cost.

## Diverting

The EU took 37% of India's 2025 bolt tonnes. Rest-of-world prices are about EUR 391/t lower. One firm can move. The sector cannot, with the US under Section 232 and the UK starting its own CBAM in January 2027.

## Trade so far

Through 2026-08, the average effect of the default burden on EU imports is not distinguishable from zero (beta -0.34, se 0.34; placebo t -0.2). A stricter version gives -0.93 (t -2.3), but its pre-2026 points are not flat, so it is not relied on. Certificates are not on sale until February 2027.

## Limits

- No exporter, mill or verifier was interviewed. The exporter is fictional.
- Plant figures are draft baselines for crude steel, read from a secondary source. They are not verified CBAM values.
- Verification cost is a press figure at its upper end. Treble it and the trigger is 186 t/yr.
- EFDA reports that forging itself sits outside the boundary. The model keeps the plant's emissions in, which is the cautious side; taking them out moves the verified-rod hit to EUR 37k.
- EFDA expects a verifier bottleneck. If verified data slips to 2029 the ranking holds.
- Coverage of 7318 and 7326 by the steel quotas is inferred; Annex I of Reg. 2026/1384 was not read.
- Carbon is flat at the latest quarterly price, not a forecast.
