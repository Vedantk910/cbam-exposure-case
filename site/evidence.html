The evidence

Each heading states the finding, and the exhibit under it shows the numbers. Where the evidence is weak or runs against the case, the text says so.

Practitioners publish the same arithmetic

Default-route cost per tonne of fasteners in 2026. The European Fastener Distributor Association's March 2026 guide prices at €80. The Crowe UK interview gives rough figures at €90.

Seven EFDA examples match to within 5 cents, and Crowe's rough figures fall within 10%. The Indian line needs a closer look. EFDA applies the carbon-steel benchmark of 1.364 to CN 7318 15 62, while the Commission's benchmark file describes that line as stainless and gives 1.154. EFDA uses 1.154 on a comparable Chinese stainless line. This case uses carbon-steel bolts throughout, so the base numbers do not depend on the answer, but I would raise it with EFDA.

Until 2030 the 2.5% reading misses most of the sector's bill

India's CBAM-scope exports to the EU at 2025 volumes on default values, EUR million a year.

The 2026 bill is of the value of that trade. Two outside estimates come in lower: FEPS-NIPFP at 16% of 2022 unit value, and Sentra.world at 10 to 20% of product cost. Neither was an input, and the FEPS study predates the published default values. Asia Research and Engagement's summary puts sector-wide cost at €3 to €4 a tonne in 2026, far below the EFDA and CO2 AI figures. I have only read that summary, which does not show the method.

Leaving works for one firm and fails for the sector

India's bolt and screw exports by destination in 2025, UN Comtrade.

One firm could sell elsewhere at about /t less. The EU takes of tonnes, though, and the US carries a Section 232 duty reported at 25 to 50%. The UK starts its own CBAM in January 2027 with no carve-out for India.

What people in the trade have said

Paraphrased from public reporting. I have spoken to none of these people.

European Fastener Distributor Association, March 2026
Importers will almost certainly be on default values for 2026, because a small pool of verifiers cannot visit thousands of plants in nine months. The association calls the defaults absurdly high and is lobbying to change them. Guide
EFDA and carboneer webinar, reported April 2026
Hendrik Schuldt of carboneer told over 200 participants that verification fails if errors exceed 5% of reported emissions. EFDA expects some fastener costs to rise by 30 to 50% in the first year. Report
Jamie McLeod, Crowe UK, February 2026
Importers should set up a process now to get actual data from suppliers, since defaults become more punitive each year and every supplier needs verifying. Interview
Swaroop Banerjee, JSW, quoted by CO2 AI, October 2026
Indian mills are largely able to produce verified emissions data. That is the supply this case depends on, and it comes from a mill. Article
Unnamed executive, Business Standard, January 2026
A senior executive at a top Indian steelmaker said the company had secured EU-recognised plant-level verification. I could only read a search summary of this report, because the page blocked access.
