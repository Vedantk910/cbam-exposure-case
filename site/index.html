The answer

A 600-tonne-a-year Indian fastener maker asks what to do about CBAM before 2034.

Buy verified rod from your current mill and verify your own plant. Start now, and stay in the EU.

Five positions on one yardstick

Present value of the hit over 2027 to 2034, measured against a Taiwanese rival on its own default values. The exporter is fictional, sized from a worked fuel example.

Why

The bill is already large. A tonne of bolts on default values carries in 2027, about of what EU buyers paid for Indian bolts in 2025. Across India's 2025 trade with the EU that is a year, against on the 2.5% reading.

Most of the saving sits in the steel rod. Verifying your own plant saves over the period. Getting a verified figure from your rod mill as well saves . The mill's report accounts for of the prize, and the exporter has to go and ask for it.

Low-carbon rod comes second. The EU gives less free allocation to cleaner routes, which takes back of the gain in 2027. At a premium it pays in 2034 once carbon passes /t, close to today's price, so the question comes back around 2030.

What the exporter should do first

  1. Ask the rod mill for its CBAM verification report on 2026 production. Any verified figure below tCO₂e/t beats the marked-up default from 2028.
  2. Line up a verifier now. The Commission expects first verification work in early 2027, the first visit has to be physical, and the European Fastener Distributor Association expects a bottleneck. Verified data pays above about tonnes a year.
  3. Write the saving into EU contracts. The importer pays CBAM and the exporter pays for verification, so the split has to be agreed.
  4. Buy as a cluster. One small exporter has little pull on a mill, while a cluster's rod order can ask for the report and share a verifier.

Does the arithmetic hold up?

The European Fastener Distributor Association publishes the same formula with eight worked examples. Seven match this model to within 5 cents a tonne. The eighth, an Indian line, matches on the association's benchmark and raises a question about which benchmark that line should carry. The full comparison is on the evidence page.
